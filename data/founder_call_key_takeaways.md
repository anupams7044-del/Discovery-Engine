# Founder Call — Key Takeaways for Graduation Project
> Source: Feedback session by Arindam Mukherjee (Sep 19, 2026, 139 minutes)

---

## 1. Segmentation — Must Be Narrow & Objective
- Segment by **need/behavior**, not demographics alone
- Must be based on **objective criteria queryable from product data**
  - ✅ "Users with ≥5 search attempts/month who have 10K+ photos"
  - ❌ "Gen Z users aged 20-29 in Tier 1 cities"
- Must include **segment sizing** (how large is this segment?)
- Must include **impact sizing** (what is the potential impact of solving for this segment?)

## 2. Problem Definition — Never Solution-Led
- ❌ "The problem is that [my solution] doesn't exist"
- ✅ "Users with 10K+ photos can't find old memories because search doesn't understand combined queries (person + place + time)"
- Must define: user segment → observed behavior → impact → evidence
- Use **Problem Framing Canvas**: cause → effect, with evidence

## 3. Metrics — Go Deeper Than Frameworks
- Don't just list North Star + Guardrails from a framework
- Must include **event-level metrics** mapped to user flows:
  - Click-through rates on specific buttons
  - Drop-off rates page-to-page
  - Where exactly users abandon a flow
- Must show **data orientation**: "How would you query/compute this metric?"
- Define each metric: name, unit, how measured

## 4. Risks & Mitigations — Depth Required
- ❌ One-line risks with one-line mitigations
- ✅ For each risk:
  - Specific failure mode
  - Root cause
  - Detection signal (how will you know it's happening?)
  - Concrete mitigation with trade-offs
  - Monitoring plan

## 5. AI Usage — Build With It, Don't Think With It
- Use AI for **building**: MVP, discovery engine, coding agents ✅
- Do NOT outsource **thinking** to AI: problem framing, segmentation, insights ❌
- Evaluators are seasoned PMs who use AI daily — they can tell
- "You're appearing immature by using [AI-generated jargon]"
- Validate, edit, and **own** the reasoning

## 6. User Research — Interviews Over Surveys for "Why"
- **Surveys** answer: "How many people face this?"
- **Interviews** answer: "Why do they face this?"
- Go deeper in interviews — don't accept surface-level answers
- Show the **specific factual examples** users share (not summaries)
- At least 5-6 user interviews for depth

## 7. Creativity of Solution — 3 Levels
1. **Problem-Solution Pairing**: Does the solution actually address the identified problem?
2. **Differentiation**: Is it different from what exists in the market?
3. **Competitive Advantage**: Does it have a moat? Can competitors copy it in a week?

## 8. Presentation — 10-Slide Deck Guidelines
- Slide title = the **single message** of that slide
- Don't restate the problem statement (it's the brief — everyone knows it)
- Evidence-forward (one idea per slide)
- Don't use AI-generated jargon without definitions
- Structure should showcase **your thought process**
- Depth over breadth: one solution taken deep > four solutions treated shallow

## 9. MVP & Usability Testing
- Deployed link must be **clickable and accessible**
- Latency under 1 minute is acceptable (free hosting is fine)
- Usability testing: 3-5 users, task-based
- Document: key findings + what you'd change in V2/V3
- This is new and won't negatively impact scores (brownie points)
- For photo testing, **AI-generated photos are acceptable**

## 10. AI Discovery Engine
- Scrape from: Play Store, Reddit (at minimum), plus optional sources
- Scale: **At least 1,000 items**, no need for 1 lakh
- Don't burn tokens unnecessarily
- Build it, make it accessible, show the workflow
- Must be **deployed and working** when evaluators click

## 11. Evaluation Process
- 3 blind evaluators per submission
- 15 criteria grouped in 4 categories
- Scores normalized across evaluators
- 70% cutoff for top fellow status
- Weightage: not equally weighted across criteria (not disclosed to prevent gaming)

---

## Critical Mistakes to Avoid (from founder)
1. Spending less than 15-16 days on the project
2. Using AI to generate slides without reviewing/understanding them
3. Broad segment definition with no sizing
4. Problem framing as "solution doesn't exist"
5. Metrics without definitions or event-level detail
6. One-line risk mitigations
7. Not deploying the discovery engine / MVP properly
8. Not doing user research (interviews, not just surveys)
9. Comparing with other submissions instead of focusing on self-improvement
10. Wasting a slide on restating the problem statement
